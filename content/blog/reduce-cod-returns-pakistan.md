For most online stores in Pakistan, cash on delivery is not optional. Many buyers will not pay in advance to a brand they have not bought from before, and turning COD off usually means fewer orders.

The problem is what happens after the order. When a customer refuses a COD parcel, you pay for the delivery, often pay again for the return, your stock is stuck in transit for days, and the product sometimes comes back damaged. A store can look busy on order count and still lose money.

You cannot remove refusals completely. You can reduce them a lot, and most of the fixes are on your own store and in your own process, not with the courier.

## Why COD parcels get refused

Before changing anything, look at your last few weeks of returned orders and sort them into reasons. Most refusals fall into a few groups:

- **The order was never serious.** An impulse tap from an ad, a test order, a duplicate, or a fake name and number.
- **The customer changed their mind while waiting.** The longer delivery takes, the more time there is to regret, find it cheaper elsewhere, or simply forget.
- **The parcel did not match what they expected.** Colour, size, fabric or quantity looked different on the product page.
- **The total was a surprise.** Delivery charges or extra fees were not clear before ordering.
- **The courier could not reach them.** Wrong address, incomplete address, phone switched off, or nobody home.
- **They never got a clear answer to a question.** A message on WhatsApp or Instagram went unanswered, so they lost trust.

Each group has a different fix. If you only do one thing from this article, do this sorting exercise first, so you fix the real problem instead of guessing.

## 1. Confirm every order before you ship it

This is the single most useful habit for COD stores. Before an order is packed, confirm it by WhatsApp message or a short call:

- Repeat the product, size, colour and quantity.
- Repeat the full total, including delivery.
- Confirm the address, including a landmark, and the best number to call.
- Mention the expected delivery time.

Orders where the customer does not reply, or replies that they did not place it, should not ship until they confirm. Shipping an unconfirmed COD order is paying for a delivery you probably will not be paid for.

Keep the message short and friendly, and send it quickly. A confirmation sent minutes after the order lands while the buyer is still interested. One sent the next day often does not.

## 2. Make the product page answer the questions people ask later

A large share of "it's not what I expected" refusals start on the product page. Check every product for:

- **Real photos,** including close-ups of fabric or texture and at least one photo in natural light.
- **Exact sizes,** with a size chart in centimetres or inches, and a note on fit if the item runs small or large.
- **What is included.** If a set has three pieces, say so. If an accessory in the photo is not included, say that too.
- **Colour honesty.** If colours can look different on screens, show the product in more than one light.

Every question a buyer has to ask on WhatsApp is a chance for them to give up. Every answer already on the page is one less reason to refuse at the door.

## 3. Show the full price before checkout

Surprises at the door cause refusals. Make the total clear before the order is placed:

- Show delivery charges on the product page or near the add-to-cart button, not only at checkout.
- If you offer free delivery over a certain amount, say the amount clearly.
- If you charge anything extra for COD, show it before the customer orders, and repeat it in the confirmation message.

## 4. Collect better addresses

Couriers cannot deliver to "near the mosque, main road". Small changes to your checkout reduce failed deliveries:

- Ask for a nearby landmark as a separate field.
- Ask for the city from a list instead of free text, so spelling mistakes do not send parcels to the wrong place.
- Ask for a phone number that will be switched on during the day, and check it is a valid Pakistani mobile number.

## 5. Ship fast and keep the customer informed

Every extra day between order and delivery gives the buyer more time to change their mind. Aim to dispatch confirmed orders the same or next working day, and:

- Send the tracking number as soon as the parcel is booked.
- Send a short message when the parcel is out for delivery, so the customer keeps the cash ready and answers the courier's call.

If you work with more than one courier, compare which one delivers faster and with fewer failed attempts in each city, and use that one for those cities.

## 6. Give people a reason to pay in advance, without forcing it

You do not have to remove COD to get more prepaid orders. Make prepaid the easier choice for the customers who are already comfortable with it:

- Offer a small discount or free delivery for prepaid orders through JazzCash, Easypaisa, card or bank transfer.
- For high-value orders, or for customers who have refused before, ask for the delivery charge in advance and keep the rest as COD. Explain why, politely, in the confirmation message.

Keep the rules simple and show them on the checkout page, not only in the policy.

## 7. Track refusals by customer, city and product

Keep a simple record of every refused order: customer phone number, city, product and reason. Over time it shows patterns:

- **Repeat refusers.** Ask them for an advance payment before you ship again.
- **Problem cities or areas.** Try a different courier there.
- **Problem products.** If one product keeps coming back, the product page or the product itself is the issue.

On Shopify, you can tag orders and customers, which makes this record easy to keep without extra apps.

## What not to do

- **Do not switch off COD overnight.** Test changes like prepaid discounts first and watch what happens to your total orders, not only your return rate.
- **Do not blame the courier for everything.** Some refusals are courier problems. Most start before the parcel ever leaves your store.
- **Do not ship to save time.** Skipping confirmation feels faster on a busy day. It is usually the most expensive shortcut a COD store can take.

## Where to start

Start with the two steps that cost nothing: **sort your recent refusals by reason**, and **confirm every order before shipping**. Then fix the product pages and checkout details that your sorted list points to.
