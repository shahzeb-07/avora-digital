Most brands ask this question the wrong way round. They start with the platform, then try to fit the business into it. The better order is: what are you selling, who is buying, and what does the site need to do on day one? Once that is clear, the choice usually makes itself.

Here is how we think about it when a brand asks us.

## The short answer

If you sell physical products and want to start taking orders soon, **Shopify is usually the right first step**. It handles products, inventory, checkout, order emails and a large app ecosystem out of the box.

If your site needs to do something Shopify was not designed for, such as a booking flow, a calculator, a members area, a 3D product view built from scratch, or a site that is mainly content and lead generation, **a custom website is usually the better fit**.

Many brands end up with both: a Shopify store for selling, and custom pages or features where Shopify gets in the way.

## What Shopify does well

- **Speed to launch.** A clean store with your products, policies and payments can go live in weeks, not months.
- **Checkout you do not have to build.** Cart, checkout, order confirmation, abandoned-cart emails and basic shipping rules already exist and are maintained by Shopify.
- **Your team can run it.** Adding products, editing prices and running a sale do not need a developer.
- **Apps for common needs.** Reviews, upsells, subscriptions, loyalty programmes and courier integrations are mostly an install away.

## Where Shopify gets expensive or awkward

- **Monthly costs add up.** You pay for the Shopify plan, and many useful features come from paid apps. Five or six apps can cost more than the plan itself. Check current prices on Shopify's own pricing page before you budget.
- **Payments from Pakistan.** Shopify's own payment system, Shopify Payments, is [not available for businesses registered in Pakistan](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries). Pakistani stores rely on third-party gateways and cash on delivery, and each gateway has its own approval process, fees and limits. This matters a lot if you want to sell abroad.
- **Themes have limits.** You can customise a theme heavily, but some layouts and interactions fight the theme's structure. That is where costs climb.
- **Apps slow stores down.** Every app adds scripts. A store with many apps often loads noticeably slower on phones.

## What a custom website does well

- **Exactly what you need, nothing extra.** No unused features, no app scripts you did not ask for. Custom sites can be very fast.
- **Unusual features.** Interactive product views, calculators, quizzes, booking flows, multi-step forms and integrations with your own systems are built to fit, not bent to fit.
- **No platform lock-in.** You own the code and can host it wherever you like.
- **Lower running costs for content sites.** A site that mainly explains your services and collects enquiries can run on low-cost hosting.

## Where a custom website is the wrong choice

- **You need a full store, fast.** Building cart, checkout, payments, stock and order management from scratch takes much longer and costs more than Shopify for the same result.
- **Nobody on your side can maintain it.** A custom site needs a developer for changes that Shopify would let your team do themselves. Plan for ongoing care.
- **Your budget is tight and your product range is simple.** A well-set-up Shopify store will almost always be cheaper to launch.

## Five questions to decide

1. **Do you need a cart and checkout on day one?** If yes, start with Shopify unless you have a strong reason not to.
2. **Is there one feature that makes your site different?** If that feature is central to how people buy, price it as custom work, either as a full custom site or a custom section inside Shopify.
3. **Who will update the site every week?** If it is your team, Shopify's admin is a big advantage.
4. **Where are your customers?** If they are abroad, check payment options for your business first. The platform choice does not fix a payment problem.
5. **What is your budget for the next 12 months, not just launch?** Add plan fees, apps, hosting and the care you will need after launch.

## A common middle path

For many brands we recommend Shopify for the store, with custom-coded sections for the parts that need to stand out: a product finder, a size guide that actually works, or a richer product page. You get Shopify's checkout and admin, with a site that does not look like every other theme.
